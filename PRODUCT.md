# PRODUCT.md: Link

## What it is

Link is a feature inside Facebook Events. You queue for an event, and Link forms a
small **squad** of people near you who'll get along. It opens a short-lived squad chat with
an AI host and helps you find each other at the venue. Afterwards, you can add each other as
friends if you both want to.

The barrier it removes is **going alone**. Plenty of young adults see an event they'd like to
attend and skip it because nobody they know is going.

## Who it's for

- Adults aged 18 to about 29 who use Facebook for Events and Groups.
- In particular: people who have just moved to a city, started a new job or school, or whose
  friends have scattered.
- They are comfortable with games and matchmaking queues. "Queue up" and "party found" are
  familiar words to them, not jargon.
- They worry about safety and awkwardness more than logistics.

Link is 18+. Age bands are still checked inside matching as a backstop.

## Demo persona

**Jordan Lee**, 23, Palo Alto. Jordan moved for a job three months ago, has a handful of
work friends and a few Groups (climbing, photo walks, coffee), and keeps tapping
"Interested" on events they never go to. Tonight it's Indie Night at The Guild Theatre in
Menlo Park. Jordan queues from the event page and gets a squad of six. The squad meets at the
front entrance, the AI host breaks the ice, and the talk cards give Jordan two things to say
to each person. After the show, Jordan adds three of them as friends.

Every person, event, post and chat in the app is seeded. The app never calls, scrapes or logs
in to Facebook.

## Voice

- **Plain and warm.** Short sentences, and second person for the user ("You're 3rd in line
  near Downtown Palo Alto").
- **Specific beats clever.** "6 people going to Indie Night together" beats "Your crew
  awaits".
- **Honest about AI and data.** Say what the model wrote, and what data it used and didn't
  use. Never imply a simulated person is real.
- **Calm on safety.** State the rule without alarm: "Squads only meet at public venues."
- **Sentence case**, no exclamation marks except the one loud moment, and no emoji in UI
  chrome. People's messages can contain anything.

## What success looks like in the demo

In five minutes a judge sees all of this:

1. A Facebook-native Events tab with one obvious way in.
2. A queue that feels like a game queue.
3. The squad-found moment.
4. An AI host and talk cards that cite their sources.
5. Finding each other at the door.
6. Mutual friending afterwards.

Throughout, it works on judges' phones live, and offline from cache if the Wi-Fi dies.
